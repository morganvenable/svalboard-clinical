1. Carpal Tunnel Syndrome
Clinical Problem
The carpal tunnel is a fibro-osseous canal formed by the carpal bones and the transverse carpal ligament. It contains:

Median nerve

Flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) tendons

Flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS) tendons

Flexor pollicis longus (FPL)

Symptoms arise from:

Increased pressure within the tunnel

Repetitive tendon gliding → synovial thickening

Sustained wrist extension or flexion

Result: median nerve compression → paresthesia, weakness, thenar atrophy

Mechanical Issue in Conventional Typing
High repetition of FDP/FDS tendon excursion

Sustained wrist extension (~20–30°) increases tunnel pressure

Finger force generation relies on extrinsic flexors crossing the wrist

Co-contraction increases compressive load inside the tunnel

Svalboard Advantage
Reduced tendon excursion → less friction within the carpal tunnel

Minimal force requirement → decreased FDP/FDS activation

Neutral wrist posture → lower intracarpal pressure

Palmar support → reduces need for wrist stabilization via extensor co-contraction

Shift toward intrinsic muscles (lumbricals/interossei):

These originate distal to the carpal tunnel

Do not increase tunnel pressure to the same degree

Clinical Summary
Svalboard reduces both dynamic friction (tendon gliding) and static compression (wrist posture + co-contraction)—the two primary drivers of carpal tunnel pathology.

2. Cubital Tunnel Syndrome
Clinical Problem
The ulnar nerve passes through the cubital tunnel at the elbow, where it is vulnerable to:

Compression (especially with elbow flexion)

Traction during repeated flexion-extension cycles

Innervates:

Interossei

Lumbricals (ulnar side)

Adductor pollicis

Intrinsic hand strength and coordination

Mechanical Issue in Conventional Typing
Sustained elbow flexion >90°

Forearm pronation and unsupported arms → increased tension on ulnar nerve

Compensation patterns:

Shoulder elevation

Wrist extension stabilization

Svalboard Advantage
Full palm support → reduces need for sustained elbow flexion

Encourages more open elbow angles

Reduces proximal stabilization demands (less trapezius + shoulder loading)

Lower force typing reduces need for ulnar-innervated intrinsic compensation under fatigue

Decreases cumulative neural tension along the ulnar nerve pathway

Clinical Summary
Svalboard primarily reduces proximal load and neural tension, addressing cubital tunnel not at the wrist—but at the elbow and shoulder chain level.

3. Muscular Dystrophy (e.g., Duchenne, Becker)
Clinical Problem
Progressive degeneration of skeletal muscle leads to:

Reduced force generation

Fatigue susceptibility

Loss of proximal stability first, then distal control

Key issue:

Energy expenditure per task becomes limiting

Mechanical Issue in Conventional Typing
Requires:

Sustained postural stabilization (shoulders, trunk)

Repeated activation of extrinsic flexors/extensors

High movement amplitude → high metabolic cost

Loss of proximal stability forces compensatory distal effort

Svalboard Advantage
Extremely low activation threshold → minimal force per keystroke

Near-zero finger travel → reduced energy expenditure

Full palm support → eliminates need for proximal stabilization

Allows typing via:

Small intrinsic muscle activations

Reduced reliance on fatigable proximal musculature

Clinical Summary
Svalboard is fundamentally an energy minimization device, allowing functional typing capacity to persist even as overall muscle strength declines.

4. Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome & Generalized Connective Tissue Disorders
Clinical Problem
Ligamentous laxity

Joint instability

Increased reliance on muscle co-contraction for stability

High fatigue and pain due to compensatory muscle overuse

Common issues:

Wrist instability

MCP/PIP hyperextension

Chronic tendon irritation

Mechanical Issue in Conventional Typing
Requires continuous joint stabilization via muscle activation

Repetitive motion through unstable joints

High reliance on extrinsic muscles crossing multiple joints

Svalboard Advantage
External stabilization via palm support

Reduces need for muscular co-contraction

Minimal joint excursion

Less stress on unstable MCP/PIP joints

Encourages short-range intrinsic muscle use

More mechanically efficient and stabilizing

Reduces shear forces across lax connective tissues

Clinical Summary
Svalboard replaces active stabilization (muscle-driven) with passive stabilization (device-supported)—a critical shift for hypermobility conditions.

5. De Quervain’s Tenosynovitis
Clinical Problem
Inflammation of the first dorsal compartment:

Abductor pollicis longus (APL)

Extensor pollicis brevis (EPB)

Caused by:

Repetitive thumb abduction + extension

Friction within a constrained tendon sheath

Mechanical Issue in Conventional Typing & Mouse Use
Repeated thumb extension/abduction (especially with trackpads/mice)

Stabilization demands from thumb during key presses

High excursion + friction at the radial wrist

Svalboard Advantage
Reduces need for thumb-driven input

Minimal thumb excursion compared to traditional devices

Lower force → reduced tendon loading

Palm support reduces need for thumb stabilization of the hand

Clinical Summary
Svalboard reduces both:

Tendon excursion

Stabilization demands on the thumb

…directly targeting the mechanism of De Quervain’s irritation.

6. Dupuytren’s Contracture
Clinical Problem
Fibrosis of the palmar fascia

Progressive finger flexion contractures (typically ring/small finger)

Not primarily inflammatory—but:

Mechanical stress may exacerbate symptoms

Extension is limited; forceful use is uncomfortable

Mechanical Issue in Conventional Typing
Requires:

Repetitive finger extension and flexion

Larger motion arcs

Can strain already restricted fascial structures

Svalboard Advantage
Reduced movement amplitude

Operates within limited ROM

Lower force requirements

Allows functional typing despite contracture

Minimizes strain on palmar fascia

Clinical Summary
Svalboard does not treat the contracture—but enables functional adaptation within restricted biomechanics.
